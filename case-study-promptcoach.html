The problem

Real estate agents needed personalized, platform-ready marketing copy, and generic AI output wasn't cutting it. Before Prompt Coach, agents either avoided AI entirely or pasted generic requests into Compass AI, our own AI embedded in the platform, and got copy that sounded nothing like them. The missing piece wasn't the AI, it was the UX layer that helped users give the AI what it needed.

Discovery: straight to the agents

One of our six was an Agent Experience Manager out of the Compass Malibu office. She connected us with agents who walked us through their frustrations firsthand: generic output, copy that didn't sound like them, prompts they didn't know how to write. Those conversations shaped the concept, and we tested the tool with the same agents before submitting.

Solution: the interface does the prompting

Prompt Coach guided agents through a structured workflow, auto-populating MLS listing data and capturing tone, audience, and brand voice upfront. By the time the AI generated copy, it sounded like the agent, not a template. The user never had to learn prompting, the interface did that work invisibly.

The interface does the prompting: a simple 4-step guided form for the agent, a fully structured, personalized prompt for the AI, invisibly assembled underneath.

What I'd take from this

Prompt Coach taught me where the real design problem in AI lives. It isn't making the model smarter; it's helping humans express intent. Agents knew exactly what they wanted, they just had no way to say it to a machine. That translation is a UX problem, and it's the lens I've brought to every AI project since.

What I'd do differently: stay closer to the feature after handoff. The 72-hour sprint was the headline, but the learning I gave up was in the long tail of how agents actually used it.
